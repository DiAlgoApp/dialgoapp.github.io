Small messages, everyday care

Did she get up all right?

She taps “Good morning” when she wakes up and everyone gets it, with its own sound. It is a simple way to start the day’s conversation.

Did she take her pill?

The app sends an alert at the time of each dose and the person marks it as taken with one tap. To add a medicine you scan the barcode on the box: DiAlgo looks it up in CIMA, Spain’s official medicines database.

Who is taking her to the doctor on Thursday?

Medical appointments are logged with date, place and notes, and the app sends reminders. The family checks the same calendar instead of asking each other.

Two different screens for the same family

A family member sets things up. The person receiving care has a simpler screen, and everyone can take part in the conversation.

For the person who needs it to be easy

  • A single screen with large buttons, each with its own colour, icon and sound.
  • If they would rather talk, they dictate the message and the app writes it.
  • They mark each dose of medication with one tap.
  • They see their upcoming medical appointments without digging through menus.

What the app looks like for an older person

For family and caregivers

  • Check-ins are shared with the family, and you can see how many relatives have read them.
  • You choose which buttons appear and in what order, and add your own (“Come over”).
  • You enter the medication and the visits; the app takes care of the reminders.
  • You reply with quick messages (“How are you?”) or by typing.
  • You export the family’s history as PDF, CSV or text.

How it helps the family

Designed around everyday communication

The person receiving care should be part of the conversation. A simpler screen gives them a more direct way to share how they feel and what they need.

  • Large buttons with text, icons and colour make sending a message simpler.
  • Conversations let people type, dictate and respond to each other.
  • The family chooses the buttons and invites the people who need to take part.

This is health data. We treat it that way.

Where it is stored
Messages, medication and visits are stored in a database hosted in the European Union (Belgium).
Who can see it
Only members of your family group, including caregivers you invite. The administrator manages invitations and permissions.
What you can do with it
Export the history whenever you like and delete your account from the app itself.
