Telecare and family communication apps: what each one offers

How a telecare service’s professional response differs from everyday communication with the family.

When an older person lives alone, the family may be looking for a response in an emergency, or for an easier way to stay in touch. These are different needs. Before choosing a tool, be clear about which one you want to cover and who will be able to respond.

What telecare offers

Depending on the service contracted, telecare can connect the person to a professional monitoring centre, provide follow-up or include other features. The equipment, hours, cost and available actions depend on the provider. Check them in the specific offer.

A pendant or a wristband may suit some people better than a touchscreen. This should be decided with the person who will use it and, where appropriate, with the professionals who care for them.

What DiAlgo offers

DiAlgo makes it easier to exchange messages between an older person, or someone who needs support, and their family. They can send “Good morning” or say how they feel, reply within the group and check the medication and appointments the family has entered.

There is no professional response centre. The “I need help” button alerts the family; it does not call 112. Notification delivery depends on the connection, the permissions and the phone. It does not locate the person or detect falls either.

Can they work together?

Yes, if each one covers a need in your situation. A family can have telecare and use DiAlgo for conversations and reminders, without mixing up the channels.

Agree on what you will do if someone asks to be contacted and gets no reply. In an emergency, use the appropriate service or call 112: do not wait for a reply in a chat.
